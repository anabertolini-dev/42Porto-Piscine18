## 🗂️ About this repository

This repository contains my work from the **42 Porto Piscine #18**.

The Piscine was my introduction to the way of learning used at 42: solving problems independently, searching for answers, testing ideas, making mistakes, debugging and trying again.

Although I already had a background in **IT and programming**, the Piscine challenged me to approach problems from a different perspective and become much more comfortable working directly with the **Unix environment and the command line**.

One of the things I appreciated the most was the **peer-to-peer learning environment**. At 42, learning does not happen only through your own code or through traditional teaching. Your peers become an important part of the process.

Discussing a problem with someone, explaining my own reasoning, seeing a completely different approach to the same exercise, or helping someone understand a concept often taught me something new as well.

This repository is therefore not only a collection of exercises. It is a record of my first experience with the **42 methodology**, including the technical challenges, the mistakes, the discoveries and the people I learned alongside.

## 💻 Piscine exercises

The Piscine was divided into different types of exercises, allowing me to progressively develop my understanding of **C programming, Shell and Unix**, while also learning how to approach problems independently.

### C exercises

Throughout the **C00 to C07** exercises, I progressively developed my understanding of C programming and problem solving.

| Project | What I practiced                                                 |
| :-----: | :--------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **C00** | Functions, character output and basic C programming concepts     |
| **C01** | Pointers, memory addresses and value manipulation                |
| **C02** | Strings, character arrays and character manipulation             |
| **C03** | String manipulation and comparison                               |
| **C04** | Type conversions, numbers and command-line arguments             |
| **C05** | Functions, recursion and algorithmic thinking                    |
| **C06** | `argc`, `argv` and program arguments                             |
| **C07** | Dynamic memory allocation and manipulation of arrays and strings |

### Rush00

**`120 / 100` — Outstanding Project**

The Rush experience was one of the most collaborative parts of my Piscine.

For our group, **Rush03 was the assigned Rush**. However, we decided to go beyond the required task and completed **Rush00, Rush01, Rush02, Rush03 and Rush04**, treating the other projects as bonus challenges.

Rush00 was developed together with **[gpraposo](https://github.com/gpraposo)** and **rpalma-l**.

Our work received a **`120 / 100`** evaluation and an **Outstanding Project** flag from a senior evaluator (*Lifeguard*).

This experience was particularly valuable because it combined **problem solving, collaboration, code review and working under time pressure**. We had to communicate constantly, divide responsibilities, understand each other's approaches and make sure the final solution worked as a whole.

Completing the other Rushes as bonus challenges also gave us the opportunity to explore different types of problems beyond the one originally assigned to our group.

## 💡 The part I found most valuable

One of the biggest differences between studying programming in a traditional environment and working through the Piscine was the amount of **independent problem solving** required.

Instead of always having a predefined path to the solution, I had to get used to:

```text
Understand
   ↓
Research
   ↓
Try
   ↓
Compile
   ↓
Break something
   ↓
Debug
   ↓
Try again
   ↓
Understand why it works
```

But independence did not mean working completely alone.

The **peer-to-peer environment** added another dimension to the learning process. Sometimes the most useful thing was not receiving an answer, but having someone ask the right question, challenge my reasoning or show me another way of looking at the problem.

Discussing an exercise with someone, explaining my own approach or seeing how another person solved the same problem often helped me understand the concept from a different perspective.

That combination of **independent research and collaborative learning** is one of the aspects of the Piscine that I want to carry forward.
